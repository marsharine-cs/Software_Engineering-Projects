THE STORY

She saw more than a calculating machine.

Ada Lovelace was an English mathematician and writer whose collaboration with Charles Babbage placed her at one of the earliest intersections of mathematics, machines, and programmable thought.

Babbage designed the Analytical Engine as a mechanical general-purpose calculating machine. Lovelace studied the design closely and recognized that its possibilities could extend beyond ordinary numerical calculation.

Her published notes explored how a machine following symbolic instructions could potentially manipulate information in broader ways—an idea that feels remarkably familiar in the age of modern computing.

THE ANALYTICAL ENGINE NOTES

Translation became something much larger.

In 1843, Lovelace published her English translation of Luigi Menabrea's description of Babbage's Analytical Engine and added extensive notes of her own.

CONCEPT 01

A programmable mechanical engine

Lovelace examined how the Analytical Engine could follow sequences of operations encoded through mechanical instructions, helping explain the machine as more than a conventional calculator.
